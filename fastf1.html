Overview

Analysis of publicly available Formula One telemetry to investigate speed-dependent performance, aerodynamic sensitivity, and stability trends. The focus is on extracting trackside-relevant insights from real session data.

Tools & Approach

Data analysis performed using Python and the FastF1 library, enabling detailed comparison of speed traces, throttle application, and cornering behaviour across laps and drivers.

Case Study: High-Speed Corner Sensitivity

Engineering Question

Where does aerodynamic behaviour limit vehicle performance at high speed?

Session Context

Free Practice 2 (FP2) representative performance runs under stable track conditions.

Key Observations

  • Reduced minimum speeds in high-speed corners relative to low-speed performance.
  • Earlier throttle lift indicating reduced driver confidence at high speed.
  • Greater variation in corner entry speed compared to slower sections.

Interpretation

The observed behaviour is consistent with aerodynamic sensitivity increasing with speed, leading to reduced platform stability and driver confidence. This suggests that limitations are primarily aerodynamic rather than mechanical grip-related.

Trackside Relevance

Improving high-speed aerodynamic robustness could enable higher minimum cornering speeds without compromising stability, directly improving lap time performance.

Engineering Insight

This type of analysis demonstrates how telemetry can be used to infer aerodynamic behaviour indirectly, supporting performance engineering decisions without direct flow-field data. It highlights the importance of integrating data analysis with aerodynamic understanding in a trackside environment.
