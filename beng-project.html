Active Front-Wing Aerodynamics

← Back

Project Overview

Final-year Aerospace Engineering project investigating the transient aerodynamic behaviour of 2026-regulation active front-wing configurations.

Objective

To assess how transient aerodynamic loads evolve during active mode transitions and evaluate their implications on vehicle stability and performance.

Methodology

CFD-based investigation progressing from simplified 2D studies to full 3D steady configurations, followed by transient simulations using k-ω SST and Detached Eddy Simulation (DES) approaches.

Key Insights

  • Transient aerodynamic load rates varied significantly between turbulence models.
  • DES captured unsteady flow physics not resolved using steady RANS methods.
  • Load-rate behaviour plays a critical role in maintaining stability during active aero transitions.

Engineering Interpretation

The results highlight the importance of considering both transient flow behaviour and model fidelity when evaluating active aerodynamic systems. These effects are especially relevant under regulation constraints where performance margins are tightly controlled.
